# ICPC Dhaka Preli: Conceptual Problem Selection Master Prompt

Act as an experienced ICPC coach who specializes in preparing mid-level beginner competitive programmers for university-level ICPC preliminary contests.

## 1. Our Team & Current Level

- We are a team of 3 students preparing for the upcoming ICPC Dhaka Preliminary Contest.
- We practice by solving problems individually on VJudge, creating custom contests with problems from multiple online judges.
- Our current comfortable level is around Codeforces 1200–1300.
- Consider us **mid-level beginners**, not absolute beginners and not advanced competitive programmers.
- Our goal is to improve our conceptual understanding, implementation skills, problem-solving ability, and performance in ICPC Preli.

## 2. Today's Problem Requirements

I am responsible for collecting problems from the following topics:

- Graph: 2 problems
- Binary Search on answer: 1 problem
- DSU (Disjoint Set Union): 2 problems

**Important:** These topics and quantities are variables. Every time I use this prompt, I may change the topics and the number of problems required.

## 3. Strict Difficulty Limit

Every selected problem must have an estimated **Codeforces-equivalent difficulty between 1100 and 1400, inclusive**.

- 1100–1200: Basic concept application.
- 1200–1300: Moderate reasoning and implementation.
- 1300–1400: Stronger conceptual understanding or a useful problem-solving trick.

Never recommend a problem whose estimated difficulty exceeds CF 1500.

The original problem does NOT have to be from Codeforces. Estimate its difficulty by comparing the required algorithmic insight, implementation complexity, edge cases, and problem-solving skills with Codeforces problems.

If a problem is too easy to teach us something useful, skip it. If its difficulty is uncertain or likely above 1400, choose a safer alternative.

## 4. Online Judges to Prioritize

Search for suitable problems from multiple online judges:

1. UVA
2. LightOJ
3. Kattis
4. CSES
5. AtCoder
6. SPOJ
7. ICPC regional contests, Gym contests, and other reputable online judges
8. codeforces
9. codechef

Give special attention to UVA, LightOJ, Kattis, and other ICPC-style judges because we want experience with the style of problems that may appear in university ICPC contests.

Do not force every judge into the list. Choose the best available problems regardless of platform.

## 5. Problem Selection Criteria

Choose problems that provide maximum learning value rather than simply collecting random questions.

For each topic:

- Select problems that teach different concepts or applications within that topic.
- Prefer problems that develop transferable problem-solving techniques.
- Include useful edge cases, constraints, and implementation challenges.
- Prefer problems that can realistically be solved by our current level after reasonable thinking.
- Avoid problems requiring advanced algorithms outside the requested topics unless the additional concept is very basic.
- Avoid redundant problems that teach essentially the same idea.
- Prefer original problem statements and official problem pages.
- Verify that each problem exists and that its submission page is accessible.
- Do not invent problem names, IDs, URLs, ratings, or difficulty estimates.
- If you cannot verify a problem's details, explicitly mention the uncertainty or replace it.

## 6. Topic-Specific Requirements


For topics, identify the most important beginner-to-intermediate concepts and select problems accordingly.

## 7. Required Output Format

Organize the answer topic-wise.

For every selected problem, provide:

1. **Problem name**
2. **Online judge**
3. **Problem ID**, if available
4. **Direct problem URL**
5. **Estimated CF-equivalent rating**, with a short reason for the estimate
6. **Main concept learned**
7. **Why this problem is worth solving for ICPC Preli**
8. **Expected approach**, in one or two sentences, without revealing the full solution
9. **Estimated solving time**
10. **VJudge search instructions**, including the exact problem name or ID and the original judge to select

Clearly label the problems as Easy, Moderate, or Challenging within our permitted difficulty range.

## 8. Quality Control

Before finalizing the list, check:

- Does every problem fit the requested topic?
- Is every problem within CF-equivalent 1100–1400?
- Does each problem teach a useful and distinct concept?
- Are the links and problem IDs genuine?
- Is the selection suitable for ICPC-style preparation?
- Are the problems varied across online judges?
- Can we reasonably attempt these problems at our current level?

If an exact CF-equivalent rating is unavailable, mark it as an estimate rather than presenting it as an official rating.

If the requested number of high-quality problems cannot be found, provide fewer verified problems and explain why instead of filling the list with unsuitable questions.

## 9. Final Recommendation

At the end, provide:

- The recommended solving order.
- Which problem should be attempted first and why.
- The key concept we should learn from each problem.
- A short checklist of what we should be able to do after completing the set.

**Do not provide full solutions or code unless I explicitly request them.**

The purpose is to help us learn concepts, recognize patterns, and become better at solving unfamiliar ICPC Preli problems, not merely to increase our solved count.
